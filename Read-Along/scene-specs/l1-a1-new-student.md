# The New Student — visual continuity and scene specifications

## Story-wide continuity bible

- **Noam:** 14 years old; slim; olive skin; short, slightly wavy dark-brown hair; brown eyes; beige knitted kippah; cream long-sleeve polo shirt with no logo; navy trousers; black school shoes; navy backpack with two shoulder straps and one small silver zipper pull. His face, build, clothes, kippah and backpack must remain identical in all four scenes.
- **Ari:** 14 years old; average build; olive skin; short black hair; brown eyes; blue knitted kippah; pale-blue long-sleeve button-down shirt; navy trousers; black school shoes. His face, build, clothes and kippah must remain identical in scenes 3–4.
- **School:** contemporary Israeli national-religious boys’ middle school built from pale Jerusalem stone. Blue-grey metal window frames, paved courtyard, modest clothing, no visible logos or written signs. The library and sports court are parts of the same campus.
- **Visual style:** cinematic storybook realism for teenagers, natural human proportions and expressions, warm but not childish, portrait composition suitable for a mobile screen.
- **Continuity arc:** the light and Noam’s body language gradually move from cool/uncertain to warm/open. Noam begins gripping both backpack straps and finishes moving freely with the group.
- **Never show:** girls in this specific boys’ school sequence, text, captions, speech bubbles, watermarks, brand marks, changing clothes, changing faces, an unexplained second backpack, distorted hands, or exaggerated cartoon expressions.

## Scene 1 — Arrival

**Sentence:** “Noam arrived at a new school, and he did not know anyone.”

**Narrative context:** This is the first instant of the story. Noam has just crossed the outer gate for his first day; no friendship or invitation has happened yet.

**Required action and objects:**

- Noam stands just inside the open blue-grey school gate.
- He wears the navy backpack correctly on both shoulders and grips both shoulder straps near his chest.
- His body is slightly still while the other pupils continue walking, showing that he does not know where to go.
- Four or five unfamiliar boys in modest school clothes walk toward the building behind him; none looks directly at him or greets him.
- The pale-stone school entrance is visible and establishes the campus used in later scenes.

**Expression and gaze:** Noam’s eyebrows are slightly raised and drawn together. His mouth is neutral. He looks sideways toward the unfamiliar courtyard—not at the camera.

**Composition:** Medium portrait shot, Noam clearly dominant in the foreground, full backpack and both hands visible, entrance and moving students readable in the background.

**Lighting:** Natural morning light, slightly cool and restrained.

**Do not show:** Noam sitting, playing, smiling broadly, talking with Ari, holding a ball, or already being welcomed.

## Scene 2 — Alone near the library

**Sentence:** “At break time, he sat alone near the library.”

**Narrative context:** Time has advanced to the first break. Noam has entered the school but has still made no connection. The isolation must be visible without making him appear bullied.

**Required action and objects:**

- The exact same Noam sits alone at one end of a wooden bench beside a large library window.
- Through the window, orderly bookshelves are clearly visible; no written “Library” sign is needed.
- His backpack has been removed from his shoulders and rests closed on the bench immediately beside his left hip. It is the same navy backpack with the same silver zipper pull.
- His hands rest loosely between his knees; he is not holding a phone, book, food or ball.
- A small group of boys talks in the middle background at a respectful distance. They are occupied with one another and are not pointing, laughing or bullying him.

**Expression and gaze:** Noam looks down and slightly toward the courtyard. His shoulders are gently rounded. He appears lonely and uncertain, not frightened or crying.

**Composition:** Medium-wide portrait shot. Noam and the backpack remain large enough to identify; library books and the distant peer group are both readable.

**Lighting:** Late-morning daylight, still somewhat cool, with softer shade beside the library.

**Continuity from scene 1:** Same face, hair, beige kippah, cream shirt, navy trousers, shoes and backpack. The only justified change is that the backpack is now on the bench.

**Do not show:** Ari approaching yet, a game invitation, a ball near Noam, broken objects, spectacles, a classroom, rain, or hostile pupils.

## Scene 3 — Ari invites Noam

**Sentence:** “Ari invited Noam, to join a class game.”

**Narrative context:** This is the turning point. It occurs moments after scene 2 during the same break. Ari has left a small game on the adjacent court and approached Noam with a clear, friendly invitation.

**Required action and objects:**

- Noam remains beside the same bench and library area, beginning to rise rather than already playing.
- His navy backpack remains closed on the bench; it must not jump back onto his shoulders.
- Ari stands one or two metres in front of Noam, smiling naturally and extending one open hand toward the game in a welcoming—not grabbing—gesture.
- Ari holds a slightly worn blue playground ball securely against his other hip.
- Three boys from the class game are visible on the nearby court in the background, leaving an obvious open place for Noam.
- Ari wears the fixed pale-blue shirt, navy trousers, black shoes and blue knitted kippah.

**Expression and gaze:** Ari looks directly at Noam. Noam looks at Ari with mild surprise and the beginning of hope. Their eye contact makes the invitation unmistakable.

**Composition:** Both faces, Ari’s open hand, the ball, Noam’s half-standing movement and the backpack on the bench must be visible. The game is secondary but legible behind them.

**Lighting:** Slightly warmer sunlight than scene 2, marking the emotional change without changing time or location.

**Continuity from scene 2:** Same bench, library wall/window and backpack position. Noam’s clothes and identity remain unchanged. Ari appears for the first time and must match scene 4 exactly.

**Do not show:** Ari pulling Noam, Noam already running, a formal sports match, a coach, trophies, cheering crowds, a different coloured ball, or mocking expressions.

## Scene 4 — Welcome through participation

**Sentence:** “One friendly invitation made Noam feel welcome.”

**Narrative context:** A few minutes after scene 3, Noam has accepted. The result of Ari’s invitation must be shown through participation and relaxed body language rather than through text or a symbolic handshake.

**Required action and objects:**

- The exact same Noam and Ari play a simple informal passing game with the same worn blue ball and two or three classmates on the same school court.
- Noam is actively receiving or gently passing the ball. The ball must be connected clearly to the group action, not lying forgotten in the foreground.
- Ari is close enough to remain identifiable and looks encouragingly toward Noam.
- The other boys naturally make room for Noam; nobody is excluded or standing as an audience.
- Noam’s navy backpack is visible in the distant background on the same bench near the library, where it was left in scene 3. It should not be on his back during the game.

**Expression and gaze:** Noam smiles with relief and concentration, shoulders open and posture energetic. Ari’s expression is friendly but natural. The pupils look mainly at one another and the ball, not at the camera.

**Composition:** Dynamic portrait action shot with Noam and Ari dominant, all limbs naturally formed, the same court and a glimpse of the library/bench establishing continuity.

**Lighting:** Warm late-morning sunlight, hopeful but realistic.

**Continuity from scene 3:** Exact same faces, ages, kippot, shirts, trousers and shoes; identical blue ball; backpack stays by the library bench.

**Do not show:** changed clothing, backpack on Noam, formal uniforms, a competitive victory celebration, medals, text saying “welcome,” adults, a different campus, or excessive hugging.
